About RentWise

How the numbers work, where they come from, and what to keep in mind

What RentWise does

RentWise compares your weekly rent with recent new leases in your postcode, so you can see if your rent is lower than usual, about usual or higher than usual. It also shows nearby areas on a map and how rents in your area have changed since 2021.

How the numbers work

Similar leases
New leases in the same postcode, with the same dwelling type and number of bedrooms, lodged in 2025.
Middle rent
Half of similar new leases cost less than this, and half cost more (the median).
Usual range
The middle half of similar new leases (from the 25th to the 75th percentile). Your rent is "lower than usual" below this range and "higher than usual" above it.
Lowest and highest
These leave out the cheapest 5% and the most expensive 5% of leases, because the true lowest and highest are often unusual cases. For example, the true lowest rent for a 2-bedroom flat in Zetland in 2025 was $243 a week, while 95% of leases were $900 or more.
Fewer than 30 leases
Results based on fewer than 30 leases are shown with a warning, because a few unusual homes can change the numbers a lot. In the trend chart, years with fewer than 30 leases are not shown.
Nearby areas
The 5 closest postcodes, within 50 km, that have at least 30 similar leases.
Rent trend
One middle rent per year, calculated from all similar new leases in that year.

Things to keep in mind

  • Only new leases. The data only includes new bonds. People who have rented the same home for years often pay less.
  • By postcode, not suburb. Rent data is published by postcode. Suburb names help you find your postcode. All suburbs in the same postcode share the same figures.
  • Lodgement dates. Dates are when the bond was lodged with NSW Fair Trading, which is usually close to the start of the lease.
  • Not live listings. The condition, location and features of a home all affect its rent. A higher rent is not always unfair.
  • Approximate boundaries. Postcode areas on the map are ABS approximations, not official Australia Post boundaries.

What the data shows

From 1,510,130 new leases in NSW, 2021–2025.

  • The middle rent for flats in NSW rose 52% from 2021 to 2025 ($460 → $700 a week). Houses rose 33%.
  • Six of the ten postcodes with the biggest rise for 2-bedroom flats are near a university. In Kingsford (2032), the middle rent went from $450 to $850. Possible reason: international students returning after COVID border closures.
  • Flat rents in Greater Sydney rose about twice as much as in the rest of NSW (+56% vs +29%).
