About RentWise

How the numbers work, where they come from, and what to keep in mind

What RentWise does

RentWise compares your weekly rent with recent new leases in your postcode, so you can see if your rent is lower than usual, about usual or higher than usual.

It also shows nearby areas where similar homes may fit your rent or budget, with links to current listings, and how rents in your area have changed since 2021.

How the numbers work

Every number compares new leases from 2025 with the same postcode, home type and number of bedrooms.

Middle rent
The typical rent: half of leases cost less, half cost more.
Usual range
Where the middle half of rents fall. Below it is "lower than usual", above it is "higher than usual".
Lowest and highest
Leave out the cheapest and most expensive 5%, which are often unusual.
Good chance
The typical rent in that area fits your budget.
Possible
Only the cheaper places in that area fit your budget.
Nearby areas
Other postcodes within 5 or 10 km, in a straight line (not travel time).
Fewer than 30 leases
Too few to be reliable, so they are shown with a warning or left out.
Rent trend
The middle rent for each year since 2021.

Things to keep in mind

  • Only new leases. The data only includes new bonds. People who have rented the same home for years often pay less.
  • By postcode, not suburb. Rent data is published by postcode. Suburb names help you find your postcode. All suburbs in the same postcode share the same figures.
  • Lodgement dates. Dates are when the bond was lodged with NSW Fair Trading, which is usually close to the start of the lease.
  • Not live listings. The condition, location and features of a home all affect its rent. A higher rent is not always unfair.
  • Listing links. "Search rentals" opens realestate.com.au or Domain with your postcode, home type and price already filled in. RentWise is not connected to these sites, and their current prices may be different from the 2025 figures here.
  • Approximate boundaries. Postcode areas on the map are ABS approximations, not official Australia Post boundaries.

What the data shows

From 1,510,130 new leases in NSW, 2021–2025.

  • The middle rent for flats in NSW (all bedroom sizes) rose 52% from 2021 to 2025 ($460 → $700 a week). Houses rose 33%.
  • Six of the ten postcodes with the biggest rise for 2-bedroom flats are near a university. In Kingsford (2032), the middle rent went from $450 to $850. Possible reason: international students returning after COVID border closures.
  • Flat rents in Greater Sydney rose about twice as much as in the rest of NSW (+56% vs +29%).
